# POPULAR PARADOX: Christian's own words

The source for every fact in the Tumble's feature (the site's rule: facts come only from Christian's Notes or his own
words). Lightly tidied from a voice note on Oct 9, 2026; nothing added.

- **Why the Tumble:** to show off the work I've done over the years, and the limitations that were there. To say you
  built a website in 1997 doesn't mean a lot to a lot of developers now. But when you look at the tools that were
  available (the tables, the basic CSS, just images and some JavaScript) and then, as time progresses, you see we've
  got more tools.
- **Fighting the browser:** as a developer it was always fighting with the browser. You had to do fixes for every
  version: Firefox, Chrome, whatever it was, Netscape and all the Mozillas and so forth. The Tumble was a good way to
  visualize how it's changed over the years.
- **The dash panel:** once I saw the dash panel and how it looked, the incorporation of the browsers themselves was
  pretty cool.
- **A time machine for the browser:** it's a time machine, but it's focused on the browser, which is one of the arch
  enemies, the nemesis, of a web developer, but also the tool that enables us to do our job. There's no web developer
  whose work doesn't get seen in a browser at some point. That's what you're building for. So there's this struggle
  between them.
- **The bubbles:** ejecting little bubbles out into the ether in this little chamber, and watching the bubbles with
  the logos go up, is a bit therapeutic.
- **Easter eggs:** there are some hidden Easter eggs in there that make it kind of fun.
- **The challenge:** a lot of this was just a challenge in design and animation, to see what we could do to make this
  dashboard functional.
