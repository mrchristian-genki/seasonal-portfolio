# Feature Magazine Template Library: Research and Proposals

Reference for building the "Feature" magazine wrappers on christiangehrke.com Field Notes. Each family below is an **original, fictional magazine** that borrows genre conventions only: voice, article formats and common layout devices. None of them copies a real title's name, logo, masthead lettering, cover trade dress or signature layout.

**Ground rules for every template**
- Facts come only from Christian's Notes or his own words. The magazine voice is costume, not new facts. Invented cover lines must point at real content in the Note.
- The narrator or byline is "GlazyArray" (a fictional staff writer works too, e.g. "by GlazyArray, Senior Field Correspondent").
- Motion rule (Oct 8, 2026): cover reveals, scorecard bars, lit "jewels", step highlights and section scrolls all ease (0.6 to 1 s, ease-in-out, scrolling via `tumbleTo`). Nothing snaps. Reduced motion is the exception.
- **Name check:** the invented names below were chosen to avoid known titles, but none has had a trademark search. Before shipping one, run a quick web and USPTO search. Parody names that echo a famous title (RIVETING TONE, POPULAR PARADOX) are fine as playful nods. Keep them clearly distinct in lettering, colour and logo shape from the title they echo.
- **Uncertain** marks a claim (mostly publication status) that I could not confirm.

## 1. Action / Adventure

**Influential titles**
- **Outside** (US, 1977): sets the standard for long-form adventure narrative mixed with gear and fitness service journalism.
- **National Geographic** (US, 1888): defines expedition photography, maps and the "explorer" persona. Its spin-off *National Geographic Adventure* ended in print in 2009.
- **The Red Bulletin** (Red Bull Media House): glossy, athlete-centred, high-energy action photography; a brand magazine.
- **Adventure Journal** (US quarterly): ad-light, literary, a "campfire stories" feel.
- **Alpinist** (US): climbing-specific, archival depth, with route topos and historical essays.

**Voice and formats**
- First-person narrative with a slow open ("At 4 a.m. the wind finally stopped"). Present tense in the action scenes, mixed sentence lengths, and understated self-deprecating humour about suffering. Jargon is light and explained in passing.
- Formats: first-person expedition narrative, "The Survivor" retold-epic, athlete profile, "Ultimate trips" list, gear guide.
- Cover-line patterns: superlatives plus danger plus the second person ("You"), and numbered lists.
  - "The Ridge That Wanted Me Back"
  - "47 Days, One Map, Zero Signal"
  - "How to Be Brave (Without Being Stupid)"

**Look**
- Headline type: big condensed sans or a heavy slab, often all caps, set over full-bleed photography. Body type is a bookish serif.
- Colour: earthy neutrals with one hot accent (safety orange, signal yellow).
- Photography: wide environmental shots with a tiny human for scale, plus tight gritty portraits.
- Signature devices:
  - route map with numbered waypoints
  - "By the Numbers" stat strip (days, km, metres gained)
  - kit list sidebar
  - pull quotes in huge type
  - chapter-style section breaks
- Density: airy openers (photo spreads) moving into dense text columns.

## 2. Drones / Aerial Video

**Influential titles.** This is a thin field for print.
- **DroneDJ** and **DroneXL**: the main English-language drone news and review sites, fast and spec-driven.
- **DPReview / Digital Camera World / PetaPixel**: camera-media reviews of camera drones (DPReview survived a 2023 shutdown when Gear Patrol bought it).
- **Fly Tribe Magazine** (2023): a community-made FPV print zine, with a DIY and freestyle culture.
- Historical: **Drone 360** (Kalmbach, around 2015, current status uncertain); **Drone** (Australia, Yaffa, last print issue December 2017).
- In practice, YouTube reviewers and the SkyPixel gallery set the visual language more than any magazine.

**Voice and formats**
- Two dialects:
  - Cinematic aerial: calm, location-led, "golden hour over the reef" lyricism.
  - FPV: loud, slangy, community in-jokes ("send it", "bando", "rip").
- Both are heavy on specs (sensor size, bitrate, wind resistance, flight time) and always add a regulatory caveat.
- Formats: review with spec box and score, "how I got the shot" breakdown, location guide (with rules and permissions), build log (FPV).
- Cover lines:
  - "Flight Time vs. Real Time: We Drained 40 Batteries"
  - "The Reveal Shot, Decoded"
  - "Above the Fog Line"

**Look**
- Type: technical sans (wide or squared, HUD-style), monospaced numerals.
- Colour: deep sky blues, dusk gradients, and an OSD-green or amber telemetry accent.
- Photography: top-down "god's-eye" abstracts and horizon reveals.
- Signature devices:
  - **telemetry overlay** (altitude, speed, battery, GPS-style readout used as decoration; never a real location)
  - flight path drawn over the image
  - spec table
  - "Settings" chip row (ISO, shutter, ND, fps)
  - before/after colour grade slider
  - "Know Before You Fly" rules box
- Density: medium; image-dominant.

## 3. Technology / Gadgets

**Influential titles**
- **Wired** (1993): the culture of technology, a bold design legacy and big-idea features.
- **The Verge** (2011): review scores, a strong graphic identity and a web-native format.
- **Popular Mechanics / Popular Science**: explainers and cutaway illustrations. PopSci's print edition ended in 2021.
- **T3 / Stuff** (UK): pure gadget-lust lists, star ratings and "Hot Stuff" product grids.
- **Ars Technica**: deep, technical, long-term testing.

**Voice and formats**
- Confident, witty and second person ("You don't need this. You'll want it anyway."). Short punchy sentences in reviews and long essays in features. Jargon is explained with analogies.
- Formats: review (spec box, good/bad bullets, score), head-to-head comparison, "Best X of the year" buyer's guide, teardown, big-idea essay, "How it works" explainer.
- Cover lines:
  - "The Gadget That Fixed My Mornings"
  - "We Opened It Up So You Don't Have To"
  - "37 Things Worth Your Money Right Now"

**Look**
- Type: geometric or grotesque sans, heavy weights, tight tracking, numerals as graphics. Wired-style bold experimental type is a common reference; do not imitate its specific masthead.
- Colour: high contrast, white or black grounds plus one neon accent per issue.
- Photography: studio product shots on seamless backgrounds, exploded views, hands-in-frame usage shots.
- Signature devices:
  - spec box
  - **Good / Bad** two-column bullets
  - score badge (x/10 or stars)
  - comparison table
  - teardown "exploded" labelled diagram
  - price tag chips
- Density: high in buyer's guides, low in feature openers.

## 4. Hiking / Outdoors

**Influential titles**
- **Backpacker** (US, 1973): trip guides, maps, gear tests, skills ("Skills" and "Survival" columns).
- **Outside**: crossover narrative.
- **Trail** and **The Great Outdoors (TGO)** (UK): route cards with grid maps, the "Hillwalking" craft.
- **Sidetracked** (UK): photo-led, literary adventure journal with a premium print feel.
- **Trail Runner**: data-forward running routes.

**Voice and formats**
- Warm, practical and companionable. Second person in guides, first person in trip reports. Honest about blisters and bad weather, with light humour. Uses specific jargon (switchback, saddle, cairn, base weight) with confidence.
- Formats:
  - route guide card (distance, elevation gain, time, difficulty, season)
  - first-person trip report
  - gear test with field notes
  - skills how-to with numbered steps
  - "Best hikes" list
- Cover lines:
  - "The Trail That Climbs Into the Clouds"
  - "Cut 5 Pounds Without Buying Anything"
  - "Rain All Day, Happy Anyway"

**Look**
- Type: rugged slab or humanist sans for headlines, readable serif body. Small caps for stats.
- Colour: forest green, ochre, sky, and topo-line beige.
- Photography: wide landscapes with the trail leading into frame, hikers from behind, macro details (boots, moss).
- Signature devices:
  - **elevation profile**
  - route map with mile markers
  - stats bar (distance, gain, time, difficulty dots)
  - "Trail Notes" sidebar
  - gear weight table
  - Leave No Trace box
  - topo-line background texture
- Density: medium. Guides are dense and tabular; narratives are airy.
- Note: for Hawaii hikes, keep trailhead details general and never imply where Christian lives.

## 5. Biking / Cycling (Road and Mountain)

**Influential titles**
- **Bicycling** (US, Hearst, 1961): broad audience, fitness and buyer's guides. Current print status uncertain.
- **Cycling Weekly** (UK, 1891): race news, tests, training.
- **Cyclist** (UK) and **Rouleur** (UK): luxury road culture; big photography, matte paper, reverent tone.
- **Pinkbike** (web, MTB): bike checks, field tests, a raw and funny comment culture.
- **Mountain Bike Action** (US): loud, test-heavy MTB print.
- Also: **Bikepacking.com** (route guides) and **Escape Collective** (road, founded 2023 after CyclingTips cuts).
- Outside Inc. ended its *Peloton* and *Beta* print editions, and *Bike* magazine closed in 2020.

**Voice and formats**
- Road: reverent, historical, sensory (suffering, cols, cobbles, espresso), long sentences, a European romance.
- MTB: irreverent, slang-heavy ("rowdy", "loamy", "steezy"), short sentences, jokes.
- Shared formats:
  - bike test (geometry table, spec list, ride impressions, score)
  - "Bike Check" (annotated photo of one bike)
  - ride/route story with a climb profile
  - training how-to
  - bikepacking route guide
- Cover lines:
  - "The Climb That Ate My Legs (and My Lunch)"
  - "Steep, Loose and Absolutely Worth It"
  - "Build the Bike You'll Ride for Ten Years"

**Look**
- Road: elegant serifs or refined sans, generous white space, muted palettes, motion-blur and race-moment photography.
- MTB: chunky condensed sans, splash colour, mud-and-dust action shots, low angles.
- Signature devices:
  - **geometry table**
  - annotated bike photo with callout lines ("Bike Check")
  - climb profile with gradient colour bands
  - Strava-style ride stats (distance, elevation, moving time; do not publish start/end locations)
  - Rating sliders: Climbing, Descending, Value
- Density: road is spacious; MTB is busy.

## 6. AI

**Influential titles**
- **MIT Technology Review**: authoritative, sceptical, policy-aware; explainer and investigation mix.
- **Wired**: culture and personalities of AI.
- **IEEE Spectrum**: engineering-grade explanation, charts.
- **Quanta Magazine**: beautiful conceptual explainers with custom illustration.
- **Distill** (distill.pub, on hiatus since 2021): set the bar for **interactive explanations** of machine learning.
- Newsletters (*Import AI*, *The Batch*) set the weekly-digest format.

**Voice and formats**
- Clear and curious, sceptical without being cynical. Analogies first, maths second. Mostly third person; the essayist "I" appears in opinion pieces. Hype is named and defused.
- Formats:
  - explainer ("How X actually works") with diagrams
  - interactive demo
  - experiment log ("I tried X for 30 days")
  - prompt/output gallery
  - interview Q&A with a builder
  - weekly digest
- Cover lines:
  - "What the Machine Saw (and What It Missed)"
  - "Inside the Black Box, Step by Step"
  - "I Let an AI Plan My Week. Here's the Diff."

**Look**
- Type: clean grotesque or neo-grotesque, often paired with a literary serif for essays. Monospace for prompts and outputs.
- Colour: either clinical white with one saturated colour, or dark mode with gradient "latent" colours (violet to teal).
- Imagery: diagrams, generative abstractions, screenshots. Avoid robot clichés. No brands or text inside AI renders.
- Signature devices:
  - **prompt/response blocks** (chat-bubble or terminal style)
  - annotated diagrams
  - "Key Terms" glossary sidebar
  - "What we know / What we don't" two-column
  - before/after or input/output pairs
  - experiment log table
- Density: medium-high, diagram-rich.

## 7. Digital Art / Illustration

  - "Pro Tip" callouts

## 8. Web Development / Design

**Influential titles**
- **Smashing Magazine** (2006): long, practical tutorials, with code samples and author bios.
- **A List Apart** (1998): essay-driven, standards-defining ("Responsive Web Design" first appeared there in 2010). Publishing has been sporadic in recent years (uncertain).
- **CSS-Tricks** (acquired by DigitalOcean in 2022): demo-driven snippets and an almanac.
- **net** magazine (UK, Future, 1994 to 2020; final issue 332, June 2020): the print web-design magazine, with "Projects" tutorials and showcases.
- **Increment** (Stripe, 2017 to 2021): beautifully designed, themed engineering issues; a model for "themed issue" software writing.
- Honourable mention: **Codrops** (experimental UI demos).

**Voice and formats**
- Generous, precise, peer-to-peer. First person plural ("Let's add a container query"). Medium sentences, code-literate. Humour is gentle and nerdy.
- Formats:
  - tutorial with code blocks and live demo
  - case study (problem, approach, result, lessons)
  - postmortem / incident write-up
  - opinion essay
  - "Showcase" of sites
  - tool roundup
- Cover lines:
  - "Ship It Slowly: An Ode to Easing"
  - "The Cascade Is a Feature, Not a Bug"
  - "From Sketch to Deploy in 9 Commits"

**Look**
- Type: clean sans or humanist serif body, monospace for code, often a playful display face.
- Colour: one brand colour, syntax-highlight colours as accents.
- Signature devices:
  - **code blocks** with filename tabs
  - embedded live demo frame
  - browser-chrome screenshot frames
  - "TL;DR" box
  - "Browser support" table
  - before/after diffs
  - Lighthouse-style score rings

- Density: high but well spaced.

## Cross-Category Building Blocks

Build these once as themeable components. Each takes CSS custom properties: `--mag-ink`, `--mag-paper`, `--mag-accent`, `--mag-display-font`, `--mag-body-font`, `--mag-mono-font`, `--mag-radius`, `--mag-rule`. Every template is then mostly a theme plus a section order.

| Block | Used by | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Masthead + cover** | all | Title lettering, tagline, issue name/date, 3 to 5 cover lines, hero image. Original logotype per template. Cover reveal fades in (eased). |
| **Byline / standfirst** | all | "By GlazyArray", dek paragraph, reading time. |
| **Pull quote** | all | Sizes: inline, full-width, oversized. |
| **Drop cap / essay body** | adventure, AI, web, travel, science | Optional margin notes / footnotes. |
| **Spec box** | tech, drones, photo, cycling, music, retro | Key–value table with optional icons. |
| **Stats bar / By the Numbers** | adventure, hiking, cycling, drones | 3 to 6 big numerals with labels. |
| **Scorecard / Verdict** | tech, drones, photo, music, retro, AI | Score as dial, stars, x/10, percent or decimal. Sub-score bars fill with an eased transition. Verdict text. |
| **Good / Bad** | tech, drones, photo, cycling | Two-column bullets. |
| **Q&A** | showcase, art, music, AI, retro | Bold question, plain answer, optional speaker portraits. |
| **Step list** | art, web, maker, AI, photo | Numbered steps with image/code slot and Pro Tip/Warning callouts. |
| **Timeline** | adventure, web (commits), travel, science, retro, showcase | Vertical or horizontal; timestamps in mono. |
| **Map + route** | adventure, hiking, cycling, drones, travel | Stylised SVG, waypoints only, no precise coordinates and never the home location. |
| **Elevation / climb profile** | hiking, cycling, adventure | SVG path drawn in slowly. Optional gradient bands. |
| **Gallery** | all image-led templates | Grid, masonry, contact-sheet and full-bleed plate modes. EXIF or credit-line captions. |

| **Annotated image** | cycling (Bike Check), music (desk), maker, art | Callout dots with leader lines; hover is a subtle glow. |
| **Comparison table** | tech, photo, cycling | Highlight the winner column. |
| **Sidebar / box** | all | Variants: Glossary, Kit List, Know Before You Go, Warning, Fact File, Did You Know, Lessons. |
| **Code / prompt block** | web, AI, retro, maker | Filename tab, line numbers, chat-bubble variant for prompts. |

## Recommended Build Order (first 5)

1. **HEAT SINK (Tech review).** Exercises the most reusable blocks: spec box, Good/Bad, scorecard, verdict, gallery. It is also the closest sibling to POPULAR PARADOX, so the two share and harden the review kit.
2. **CONTOUR LINES (Hiking).** Adds stats bar, map and elevation profile, which CONTOUR LINES, DROPOUT/COL & COBBLE, UNTETHERED and LAYOVER then reuse.
3. **SOURCE ORDER or 200 OK (Web dev).** Adds code blocks, step list, timeline and before/after. Christian's own builds (the Tumble, the parts catalog) are natural first case studies.
4. **STUDIO FLOOR (Showcase profile).** Adds Q&A, annotated image and the low-density gallery, and the RIVETING TONE profile lessons carry over directly.
5. **HIDDEN LAYER or PROMPT & CIRCUMSTANCE (AI).** Adds prompt/response blocks and glossary sidebars. After this, nearly every remaining template is a theme plus a section order, with no new components.

After the first five, the cheapest wins are COL & COBBLE / BERM BURNER (reusing the hiking blocks), LINE OF SIGHT (spec plus before/after plus a HUD overlay) and BLINKING CURSOR (code block plus scorecard in retro dress).
