Whale Beach at sunset

A sunset ride down to Whale Beach on Lake Tahoe's east shore, with his wife and Marley walking down. An hour on the beach, polished carnelian in the sand, and Marley swimming the whole time.

The map shows 3.8 mi of the 4.2 mi; the numbers count all of it.

Whale Beach is one of our favourites, and the name is honest: the big granite boulders in the shallows really do look like a pod of whales surfacing.

On a Saturday evening in late March my wife wanted to walk down with Marley, so I rode. It's about two miles and 350 feet down from the road to the water, and I was at the lake by six.

We spent an hour on the beach. It was warm for March, and Marley swam the whole time. Down at the water's edge there's carnelian in the sand, orange and amber pebbles polished smooth by the lake.

I put the drone up at seven to get the beach from above, and a few minutes later, with the sun low over the mountains on the far shore, we took the obligatory sunset selfie.

Then the ride back up: 4.2 miles all in, 370 feet of climbing, and only about 25 minutes of it actually moving.

Afterwards I took the sunset photo of the rocks and let an AI video tool imagine what the name suggests. That clip is made with AI. The rocks, the light and the evening were real.

What I learned: some places earn their names. And an hour like that is worth the ride back up.
